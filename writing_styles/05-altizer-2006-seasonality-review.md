# 05 — Altizer et al. (2006), *Ecology Letters*
**"Seasonality and the dynamics of infectious diseases"**
Altizer S, Dobson A, Hosseini P, Hudson P, Pascual M, Rohani P. *Ecol Lett* 9(4):467–484. doi:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2005.00879.x.

**Archetype:** the *mechanism-inventory review* — an ecological review that takes one driver (seasonality) and enumerates the biologically distinct mechanisms through which it acts, then draws out the consequences for dynamics and for control. The natural companion to [[14-baker-2021-infectious-disease-global-change]] and [[15-heesterbeek-2015-models-global-health]].

## The abstract, verbatim
> "Seasonal variations in temperature, rainfall and resource availability are ubiquitous and can exert strong pressures on population dynamics. Infectious diseases provide some of the best-studied examples of the role of seasonality in shaping population fluctuations. In this paper, we review examples from human and wildlife disease systems to illustrate the challenges inherent in understanding the mechanisms and impacts of seasonal environmental drivers. Empirical evidence points to several biologically distinct mechanisms by which seasonality can impact host–pathogen interactions, including seasonal changes in host social behaviour and contact rates, variation in encounters with infective stages in the environment, annual pulses of host births and deaths and changes in host immune defences. Mathematical models and field observations show that the strength and mechanisms of seasonality can alter the spread and persistence of infectious diseases, and that population-level responses can range from simple annual cycles to more complex multiyear fluctuations. From an applied perspective, understanding the timing and causes of seasonality offers important insights into how parasite–host systems operate, how and when parasite control measures should be applied, and how disease risks will respond to anthropogenic climate change and altered patterns of seasonality. Finally, by focusing on well-studied examples of infectious diseases, we hope to highlight general insights that are relevant to other ecological interactions."

## What the abstract teaches
This is a near-perfect **review abstract skeleton**, and the seven sentences map onto seven jobs:

1. **Situate the driver in general ecology, not in your subfield.** "Seasonal variations in temperature, rainfall and resource availability are ubiquitous and can exert strong pressures on population dynamics." Nothing about disease yet.
2. **Justify the choice of system as an exemplar.** "Infectious diseases provide some of the best-studied examples of the role of seasonality in shaping population fluctuations." The system is chosen because it is *tractable*, which pre-answers "why disease?".
3. **State scope and the honest framing.** "we review examples from human and wildlife disease systems to illustrate the challenges inherent in understanding the mechanisms and impacts…" Note "challenges", not "advances" — the review promises to expose difficulty rather than to declare a solved problem.
4. **The mechanism inventory — the core contribution.** "several biologically distinct mechanisms … including seasonal changes in host social behaviour and contact rates, variation in encounters with infective stages in the environment, annual pulses of host births and deaths and changes in host immune defences." Four mechanisms, each at a different level of biological organisation (behaviour, environment, demography, immunity). **"Biologically distinct" is the load-bearing phrase**: a review's value is in separating things that a single phrase ("seasonality") had conflated.
5. **What the two kinds of evidence jointly show.** "Mathematical models and field observations show that…" — models and data are named as co-equal sources, in that order, which is itself a claim about the field.
6. **The applied payoff, in three specific questions.** "how parasite–host systems operate, how and when parasite control measures should be applied, and how disease risks will respond to anthropogenic climate change." Note "how and when" — timing is the applied consequence of a seasonality review, and the abstract says so.
7. **The generalisation, offered modestly.** "we hope to highlight general insights that are relevant to other ecological interactions."

## Framing conventions to take from this paper
- **Enumerate mechanisms, and insist they are distinct.** The most useful thing a review can do is replace one word with a numbered list of processes that behave differently.
- **Order the list by level of organisation** (behaviour → environment → demography → immunity) so the reader can see it is a partition, not a heap.
- **Range statements as findings.** "population-level responses can range from simple annual cycles to more complex multiyear fluctuations" — a review's result is often the *range* of outcomes and what determines position within it.
- **Hedged collective voice**: "we review", "we hope to highlight". Reviews claim illumination, not proof.
- **Close on transfer**: the last sentence sells the review to readers who do not work on infectious disease at all.
