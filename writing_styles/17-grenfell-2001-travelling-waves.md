# 17 — Grenfell, Bjørnstad & Kappey (2001), *Nature*
**"Travelling waves and spatial hierarchies in measles epidemics"**
*Nature* 414(6865):716–723. doi:10.1038/414716a.

> **Basis of this analysis.** The full text is paywalled with no reachable open-access copy (checked via PMC, Europe PMC, Unpaywall, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and the authors' institutional pages, September 2026). This file is built on the **verbatim abstract** and the bibliographic record.

**Archetype:** the *pattern-detection-plus-mechanism* paper — find a spatio-temporal pattern in an exceptional dataset using a method that respects the data's non-stationarity, then reproduce the pattern with a mechanistic model and say what the pattern requires about population structure.

## The abstract, verbatim
> "Spatio-temporal travelling waves are striking manifestations of predator–prey and host–parasite dynamics. However, few systems are well enough documented both to detect repeated waves and to explain their interaction with spatio-temporal variations in population structure and demography. Here, we demonstrate recurrent epidemic travelling waves in an exhaustive spatio-temporal data set for measles in England and Wales. We use wavelet phase analysis, which allows for dynamical non-stationarity—a complication in interpreting spatio-temporal patterns in these and many other ecological time series. In the pre-vaccination era, conspicuous hierarchical waves of infection moved regionally from large cities to small towns; the introduction of measles vaccination restricted but did not eliminate this hierarchical contagion. A mechanistic stochastic model suggests a dynamical explanation for the waves—spread via infective 'sparks' from large 'core' cities to smaller 'satellite' towns. Thus, the spatial hierarchy of host population structure is a prerequisite for these infection waves."

## What the abstract teaches
Seven sentences, and the shape is the classic Nature abstract for an empirical-plus-model paper:

1. **General phenomenon, in ecological rather than epidemiological terms.** "Spatio-temporal travelling waves are striking manifestations of predator–prey and host–parasite dynamics."
2. **The gap, stated as a *data* limitation rather than an intellectual one.** "However, few systems are well enough documented both to detect repeated waves and to explain their interaction with spatio-temporal variations in population structure and demography." This is the strongest kind of gap statement when you hold an exceptional dataset: nobody has done it because nobody could.
3. **"Here, we demonstrate…" plus the dataset's defining adjective.** "an exhaustive spatio-temporal data set for measles in England and Wales."
4. **The method, named, with the specific difficulty it solves.** "We use wavelet phase analysis, which allows for dynamical non-stationarity—a complication in interpreting spatio-temporal patterns in these and many other ecological time series." **A method sentence that names the assumption other methods make and that this one does not** is worth far more than a method sentence that only names the method. The trailing clause ("and many other ecological time series") sells the method beyond this paper.
5. **The empirical result, with the intervention as a natural experiment.** "In the pre-vaccination era, conspicuous hierarchical waves of infection moved regionally from large cities to small towns; the introduction of measles vaccination restricted but did not eliminate this hierarchical contagion." Note the precision of "restricted but did not eliminate" — a partial effect reported exactly.
6. **The mechanistic explanation, hedged with "suggests" and made vivid with metaphor.** "A mechanistic stochastic model suggests a dynamical explanation for the waves—spread via infective 'sparks' from large 'core' cities to smaller 'satellite' towns." The quoted metaphors (sparks / core / satellite) are the paper's memorable vocabulary; quotation marks on first use mark them as deliberate coinages rather than loose language.
7. **The general conclusion, as a necessary condition.** "Thus, the spatial hierarchy of host population structure is a prerequisite for these infection waves." Not "is associated with" — "is a prerequisite for". Having established the mechanism, the paper claims necessity.

## Framing conventions to take from this paper
- **Justify a method by the assumption it relaxes.** "which allows for dynamical non-stationarity" earns more trust than any amount of description.
- **Use a policy change as a natural experiment on dynamics**, and report the effect as partial where it is partial.
- **Coin a small vocabulary and mark it** ('sparks', 'core', 'satellite'). Two or three quoted terms, defined once, will carry the paper into other people's citations.
- **Escalate the final sentence to a structural claim** about what the system requires, not just what it does.
