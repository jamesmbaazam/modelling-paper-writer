# 47 — Ngonghala et al. (2020), *Mathematical Biosciences*
**"Mathematical assessment of the impact of non-pharmaceutical interventions on curtailing the 2019 novel Coronavirus"**
Ngonghala CN, Iboi E, Eikenberry S, Scotch M, MacIntyre CR, Bonds MH, Gumel AB. *Math Biosci* 325:108364. doi:10.1016/j.mbs.2020.108364.

**Archetype:** *scenario projection with analytic foundations* — a compartmental model is specified, its equilibria analysed by theorem, then parameterised and simulated across intervention scenarios. The corpus's exemplar of the **applied-mathematics journal register**, where a stability proof precedes the epidemiology and the two are expected to appear in the same paper.

## Structure
Elsevier numbered sections: `Abstract` → `1. Introduction` → `2. Model formulation` → **`3. Results`** split into **`3.1 Analytical results: Asymptotic stability analysis of disease-free equilibrium`** and numerical/simulation subsections → `4.` scenario assessments → `5. Discussion and conclusions`.

**The defining structural feature: analytical results and simulation results are both "Results", in that order.** A mathematical biosciences paper establishes what can be proved before it shows what can be computed, and labels them as two kinds of the same thing. In a general-science or clinical venue the proof would be an appendix; here it is §3.1.

**Large matrices go in a `Box`**, not inline, so the derivation can be followed without the page breaking up — the next-generation matrices F and V are displayed in `Box I` and referenced from the text.

## Opening move
> "A pandemic of a novel Coronavirus emerged in December of 2019 (COVID-19), causing devastating public health impact across the world. In the absence of a safe and effective vaccine or antivirals, strategies for controlling and mitigating the burden of the pandemic are focused on non-pharmaceutical interventions, such as social-distancing, contact-tracing, quarantine, isolation, and the use of face-masks in public."

Burden, then the constraint that makes the question live (no vaccine, no antiviral), then the intervention set enumerated — and that same enumerated set structures the entire paper's scenarios.

**The abstract states the analytic result in the same breath as the simulations**, which is the register's signature:
> "Rigorous analysis of the model shows that the disease-free equilibrium is locally-asymptotically stable if a certain epidemiological threshold, known as the reproduction number (denoted by ℛ_c), is less than unity."

Note "a certain epidemiological threshold, known as the reproduction number" — the mathematical object is named first and glossed epidemiologically immediately. **Write for the reader who has one of the two vocabularies, not both.**

The Introduction dates and quantifies the situation precisely, which is what makes a 2020 COVID-19 paper readable afterwards: "As of April 14, 2020, COVID-19… has spread to over 210 countries and territories, causing about 1.9 million infections and 125,000 deaths… The first documented confirmed case of the novel coronavirus in the US (a resident who had recently visited Wuhan city in China) was reported on January 20, 2020, while the first confirmed case in the state of New York was reported on March 1, 2020."

## Methods
- **State the theorem formally, then gloss it epidemiologically, then say what it does and does not cover.** This three-part pattern is the most transferable thing in the paper:
  > "**Theorem 3.1** The disease-free equilibrium (DFE) of the model (2.1) is locally-asymptotically stable if ℛ_c < 1, and unstable if ℛ_c > 1."

  then the gloss:
  > "The quantity ℛ_c is the control reproduction number of the model (2.1). It measures the average number of new COVID-19 infections generated by an average infected individual introduced into a population where basic public health interventions (quarantine, isolation, social-distancing, testing etc.) are implemented."

  then the consequence in plain terms: "The epidemiological implication of Theorem 3.1 is that a small influx of COVID-19 cases will not generate a COVID-19 outbreak if the control reproduction number (ℛ_c) is less than unity."
- **Decompose the threshold into interpretable parts**, so a reader can see which route contributes what: "The quantity R_c is the sum of the constituent reproduction numbers associated with the number of new COVID-19 cases generated by symptomatically-infectious humans (ℛ_c^u), hospitalized/isolated individuals (ℛ_c^h) and asymptomatically-infectious humans (ℛ_c^a)." **A decomposed R₀ is an intervention map**: each term names a transmission route a policy could target.
- **Name the standard machinery rather than re-deriving it**: "The asymptotic stability of the DFE will be analyzed using the next generation operator method", with the result attributed — "The result below follows from Theorem 2 of [ref]".
- **Flag the structural caveat attached to the model class**, where the mathematics is subtle: the note that for "Kermack–McKendrick-type mathematical models with no vital/demographic dynamics… or waning immunity (to continuously feed the susceptible class)" the equilibrium structure differs — the model "has a line of disease-free equilibria", not a single point.
- **Fit to deaths, and say so plainly**, with the fitted values listed to four decimals and their units: "a least squares fit of system (2.1) to New York state (a), and entire US (b), COVID-19 related death data… The red dots represent data points, while the solid blue lines represent predictions of death from system (2.1)."

## Results
**Express each scenario as a percentage change in a model parameter**, so the policy and the model are connected explicitly:
> "strict compliance to the statewide lock-down in New York state (which corresponds to reducing the baseline contact rate in our model by at least 40%) will reduce the peak values for hospitalizations/isolation of confirmed cases and mortality by 92% and 80%, respectively."

The parenthesis is the whole craft: *this policy means this parameter change in this model*. Without it, "strict social distancing" is not a modelled quantity. (Compare the contact-matrix scenario tables in `examples.md` §12.13.)

**Report both settings in parallel, with the same two outcomes**, so the comparison is legible: New York peaks "around mid April, 2020 (with 66,300 number of hospitalizations/isolation of confirmed cases and 105,100 deaths at the peak)", the US "around end of April, 2020 (with 115,000 hospitalizations/isolation of confirmed cases and 164,000 deaths at the peak)".

**Place your projection against the other published projections, in both directions**:
> "Our projections for baseline (worst-case) mortality for the US are much lower than the 2.2 million deaths suggested by Ferguson et al. in the absence of interventions, but fall within the range estimated in [ref]."

Lower than one benchmark, inside the range of another — stated without claiming superiority.

**The headline is a timing threshold, not an effect size.** The policy result is about *when* an intervention ends, with the counterfactual quantified:
> "relaxing or terminating the strict social-distancing measures in the state of New York and the US as a whole by end of April 2020 will trigger a devastating second wave… In particular, up to 144,000 and 156,000 cumulative deaths will have been recorded in the state of New York and the entire US if the social-distancing measures are shut down by the end of April, 2020. Extending the termination of social-distancing, such as to end of June 2020, significantly reduces the likelihood of a second wave."

**Give an unconditional recommendation only where the analysis supports one**: "the use of low efficacy face masks with high coverage is always useful" — and then the interaction: "combining the face-masks use strategy with a strategy based on the implementation of strict social-distancing is more effective in curtailing (and eliminating) COVID-19, in comparison to the singular implementation of either strategy."

## Literature
Numbered Elsevier citations, clustered densely on factual claims (four or five references on a single case count). Other models are cited as comparators for the projections rather than reviewed. Real-world evidence is brought in to corroborate the central timing claim: "countries that have recently relaxed these measures, such as Taiwan, Hong Kong and South Korea, have already started seeing a rebound of COVID-19."

## Voice
First-person plural and passive in roughly equal measure — "We develop a new mathematical model", "the model was used to assess", "it was shown that". The register is more assertive than the general-science corpus papers: "will reduce", "will trigger a devastating second wave", "undoubtedly effectively combat". Where `SKILL.md` §3.4 asks for projections to be hedged as conditional on assumptions, this paper frequently does not — a reader adopting this register should keep the structural habits (decomposed threshold, parameter-mapped scenarios, theorem-then-gloss) and **soften the modals**, which is what the policy-facing corpus papers do.

The caution is instead carried in a direct instruction to decision-makers: "a great deal of caution must be exercised before decisions are made to relax or terminate the existing highly-successful social-distancing protocols."

## Discussion and limitations
`5. Discussion and conclusions` restates the situation, the model's purpose and its parameterisation, then walks the scenario results in order and closes on the timing warning. The limitations are thin by the standards of the rest of this corpus — the model's assumptions are not collected and interrogated, and no direction of bias is given for the omissions. **This is the clearest illustration in the corpus that journal register and good practice are not the same thing**: the analytic rigour in §3.1 is exemplary and the limitations discussion is not, and a writer should take one and not the other.

## Distinctive moves to borrow
1. **Prove, then gloss, then state the epidemiological implication.** Theorem, plain-language meaning of the quantity, and what it implies for an outbreak — three consecutive moves.
2. **Decompose the control reproduction number into route-specific terms**, so the threshold doubles as an intervention map.
3. **Map each policy scenario onto an explicit parameter change** — "which corresponds to reducing the baseline contact rate in our model by at least 40%".
4. **Name the standard analytic machinery and attribute the general result** rather than re-deriving it.
5. **Put large matrices in a Box** so the argument stays readable.
6. **Report two settings in parallel with the same outcomes** at the same points.
7. **Position your projection against named alternatives in both directions**, without claiming superiority.
8. **Make the headline a timing threshold** where timing is the decision, and quantify the cost of acting too early.
9. **Separate analytical and numerical results under one `Results` heading**, in that order, when writing for a mathematics venue.

## What to avoid from this paper
- **Unhedged future-tense projections.** "will trigger", "undoubtedly" — the corpus's policy papers use *could*, *is projected to*, *under the assumptions stated* (see [12-ferguson-2006-mitigating-pandemic](12-ferguson-2006-mitigating-pandemic.md), [25-davies-2020-npi-uk](25-davies-2020-npi-uk.md)).
- **A conclusions section without a limitations paragraph.** Even a short one naming the fixed parameters, the omitted age structure and the assumption of homogeneous mixing would have made the projections easier to use.
- **No uncertainty intervals on projected deaths.** Point projections of cumulative mortality are given without credible or prediction intervals; §3.1 of `SKILL.md` requires an interval matched to the object.
