# 14 — Baker et al. (2021), *Nature Reviews Microbiology*
**"Infectious disease in an era of global change"**
Baker RE, Mahmud AS, Miller IF, Rajeev M, Rasambainarivo F, Rice BL, Takahashi S, Tatem AJ, Wagner CE, Wang L-F, Wesolowski A, Metcalf CJE. *Nat Rev Microbiol* 20(4):193–205. PMC8513385.

**Archetype:** the *synthesis review* — no new results; the contribution is a framework that organises a scattered literature. Useful as the model for introductions, discussion sections and any writing aimed past your own subfield.

## Structure
`Introduction` → `Pathogen emergence into human populations` → `Local-scale disease dynamics` → `Global spread` (→ *International travel*, *Migration and local mobility*, *Intensification of animal and plant trade* → *Plant trade*, *Animal and animal-product trade*) → `A new era of infectious disease` (the outlook) → Acknowledgements → **Glossary** → Author contributions → 157 references.

The section order *is* the framework: the three steps a pathogen must complete (spillover → local establishment → global spread), each mapped to the global changes acting on it. Section headings are noun phrases naming a process, not findings.

## Opening move
> "The twenty-first century has witnessed a wave of severe infectious disease outbreaks, not least the COVID-19 pandemic, which has had a devastating impact on lives and livelihoods around the globe. The 2003 severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus outbreak, the 2009 swine flu pandemic, the 2012 Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus outbreak, the 2013–2016 Ebola virus disease epidemic in West Africa and the 2015 Zika virus disease epidemic all resulted in substantial morbidity and mortality while spreading across borders to infect people in multiple countries."

Pattern: **general claim → the enumerated roll-call that substantiates it → the framework the review will impose on it.** The list is dated and specific; it does the work that a citation alone would not.

## Methods — explaining models without equations
Four devices worth copying wholesale:
1. **The process sentence as a conceptual model.** "For a novel pathogen to become a threat to human populations, first, contact between humans and the animal reservoir must occur; the pathogen must either have or evolve … the capacity for human-to-human transmission; and finally, this human-to-human transmission must enable expansion of the pathogen's geographical range beyond the zone of spillover." Three clauses, three later sections.
2. **The figure caption as the only place mathematics appears.** "An example susceptible (*S*), infected (*I*), recovered (*R*) model is shown, where *β* represents the transmission rate and *γ* is the recovery rate." No derivation anywhere in the text.
3. **Boxes for anything that would break the narrative** (Box 1 on host and pathogen evolution), written in plain declaratives: "Mutations constantly arise in the genomes of all species, from viruses to elephants."
4. **A hyperlinked glossary** — technical terms defined inline on first use and collected at the end.

## Results — evidence synthesis
Numbers are used to *establish a trend*, with the source institution named rather than only cited:
> "The total number of airline passengers doubled from just below two billion in 2000 to more than four billion in 2019."
> "Genetic analyses demonstrate multiple introductions of SARS-CoV-2, driven by air travel, in the Middle East, northern California and Brazil."

Conflicting evidence is neither hidden nor adjudicated prematurely:
> "However the role of urbanization in vector-borne disease spread is complex: the preference of the *Anopheles* spp. vector for rural environments may have led to a decline in the prevalence of malaria in urbanizing regions. Nevertheless, dense and highly connected urban areas are potential hot spots for the rapid spread of diseases such as COVID-19 and SARS."

Absence of evidence is stated as such, with a mechanistic explanation for the absence:
> "There has been relatively little documented evidence of the introduction of new vectors via air travel. This is likely due to the low probability of vectors surviving the flight, and disembarking in a suitable region, in sufficient numbers to establish and drive an epidemic."

## Literature
~157 numbered references; most sentences carry 1–2, synthesis paragraphs 5–8. Disagreement is handled with an explicit "the role of X is complex" sentence followed by both sides — never by picking a winner in a review.

## Voice
Present tense for mechanisms, past for events, future for projections ("Climate change is expected to lead to an expansion of these tropical patterns…"). Active and agent-centred: "Recent global changes have affected each of these steps" rather than *each of these steps has been affected by…*. "We" is reserved for authorial framing moves: "In this Review, we consider the extent to which…", "Here, we review how…".

Hedging is calibrated to evidence type — "may", "could", "likely", and for inference from thin data: "It is hypothesized that the spillover of Nipah virus from bats to pigs was driven by three factors … it is hard to pinpoint the pathway".

## Discussion and limitations — the Outlook section
The closing section is titled as a claim ("A new era of infectious disease") and runs: progress → the residual threat → the counterpoints → institutional response → research agenda.
> "In recent decades, declines in mortality and morbidity, particularly childhood mortality, have been one of the great triumphs of public health."
> "Climate change, rapid urbanization and changing land-use patterns will increase the risk of disease emergence in the coming decades … However, there are counterpoints to this trend: the rapid growth of connectivity observed in the early twenty-first century may stabilize, and structural changes wrought during the COVID-19 pandemic may persist."
> "A changing world requires changing science to evaluate future risks from infectious disease. Future work needs to explicitly address concurrent changes…"

Note that the review **argues against its own thesis** before closing. That is what stops the outlook reading as advocacy.

## Distinctive moves to borrow
1. **Name the scope you are excluding.** "We do not explicitly address economic change; however, economic changes … are crucial drivers of these three factors."
2. **Triads for coverage**: "climate change, rapid urbanization and changing land-use patterns"; used consistently so the reader can track completeness.
3. **Historical framing turns urgency into acceleration, not novelty** — premodern, maritime and modern eras placed side by side.
4. Verbs of association, not causation: "have altered", never "caused".
