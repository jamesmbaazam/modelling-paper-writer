# 20 — Nouvellet et al. (2015), *Nature*
**"The role of rapid diagnostics in managing Ebola epidemics"**
*Nature* 528:S109–S116. doi:10.1038/nature16041. PMC4823022.

**Archetype:** the *intervention-evaluation with a formal strategy comparison* — two models at two scales, three candidate strategies, evaluated on several metrics, with the answer explicitly context-dependent.

## Structure
Abstract → opening paragraph → `Methods` (*Potential impact of RDTs in a health-care unit*; *Potential impact of RDTs at the population level*) → `Results` (**the same two subheadings, in the same order**) → `Discussion` → three Supplementary Information documents.

Mirroring the Methods and Results subheadings is the paper's structural signature: each analysis is specified and then answered under an identical heading, so the reader can read either section alone.

## Opening move
> "Ebola emerged in West Africa around December 2013 and swept through Guinea, Sierra Leone and Liberia, giving rise to 27,748 confirmed, probable and suspected cases reported by 29 July 2015. Case diagnoses during the epidemic have relied on polymerase chain reaction-based tests. Owing to limited laboratory capacity and local transport infrastructure, the delays from sample collection to test results being available have often been 2 days or more."

Pattern: **epidemic scale in exact counts → the operational status quo → the specific operational bottleneck.** The paper's whole argument lives in that third sentence: a two-day delay is a modellable quantity.

## Methods
- Two models at two scales, each with a clearly separate purpose: a health-care-unit model (what happens to a patient and to nosocomial transmission) and a population model (what happens to the epidemic).
- Notation kept to what is needed: R_HU defined in words on first use — "the average number of secondary infections generated by these patients".
- Baseline parameters given inline where they matter: "At baseline we assume β = 0.15 per day"; the rest in a table of scenario comparisons.
- **Simplifications are declared with their justification**: "For analytical tractability, we model a single generation of infection."
- Calibration described in one sentence: "Assuming no use of RDTs, transmission parameters are calibrated to match the time series of observed incidence (confirmed and probable cases) and bed occupancy in Sierra Leone."
- Sensitivity analysis re-runs everything under more pessimistic and more realistic test performance: "we repeat the simulations using arguably more-realistic assumptions, regarding test sensitivity and specificity. We assume PCR test sensitivity and specificity of 85% and 95%, respectively…"

## Results
Effects as **percentage reductions relative to the status quo strategy**, with the counterfactual named explicitly:
> "We estimate that adopting an RDT-only strategy would have reduced the size of the epidemic by 6%, despite imperfect sensitivity…"
> "However, with the limited sensitivity and specificity of existing RDTs, adopting a dual strategy would have been more effective than relying on either PCR- or RDT-testing alone. We estimate that such a dual strategy could have reduced the size of the epidemic by 32% compared with the PCR-only strategy."
> "If such tests had been available throughout the recent epidemic, we estimate, for Sierra Leone, that their use in combination with confirmatory polymerase chain-reaction testing might have reduced the scale of the epidemic by over a third."
> "We estimate that 37% of people with Ebola sought health care at the start of the epidemic, increasing to 41%, 62% and 73% at the beginning of October 2014, November 2014 and January 2015, respectively."

Modal verbs are graded to match evidential strength: *would have* (under stated assumptions) → *could have* → *might have* (under a hypothetical technology). This gradation is deliberate and worth imitating.

## Literature
Numbered superscripts; moderate in background and discussion, sparse in methods. Prior consensus is named and then displaced:
> "Until now, Ebola outbreaks were thought to be easily controlled. The usual narratives surrounding the control of Ebola highlight the importance of safe funerals, prompt isolation and effective contact tracing. However, testing strategies can also have a crucial role…"

The move is: *state the received view as a view*, then add rather than demolish.

## Voice
Present for mechanisms, past for the epidemic. Active first-person plural: "we develop", "we use", "we estimate", "our models", "our results". Hedging attached to specific uncertainties: "arguably more-realistic assumptions", "it is unlikely that the high nominal sensitivity and specificity … was achieved", "should be cautiously interpreted".

## Discussion and limitations
> "Our models inevitably simplify the complex processes underlying Ebola transmission dynamics and patient testing and treatment. However, they provide an important initial evaluation of the potential impact of RDT use on outbreak control."

Then the specifics: unresolved variation in reported RDT sensitivity (78–100%), unknown case ascertainment, undocumented changes in funeral practice, the single-generation simplification. Each is followed by a statement of whether the qualitative conclusion survives — the conclusions are said to be "robust to sensitivity analyses", which is a claim the sensitivity section has already earned.

## Data, code and funding — and tools
No repository, but the model is shipped as a usable artefact:
> "We implemented the health-care-unit-level model as an Excel spreadsheet and Java program (see Supplementary Information). This freely available software allows the reader to explore the impact of different model assumptions and parameter values on model outcomes."

## Distinctive moves to borrow
1. **Fix a small set of named strategies** (PCR-only, dual, RDT-only) and evaluate every metric across all of them — the reader gets a decision table, not a verdict.
2. Make the answer context-dependent on purpose, and say what it depends on: stage of epidemic, bed capacity relative to demand.
3. Name the trade-off explicitly: "a trade-off exists between the risk of nosocomial infection and the benefits of treatment."
4. Grade your modals to your evidence.
